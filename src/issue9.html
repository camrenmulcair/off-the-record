Blockade, Meet the Jerry Can: The US Navy vs. Iran's Pakistan Back Door

14 tankers are massing at Kharg Island, and Tehran says the first shot puts the Gulf's oil in play.

TEHRAN — CENTCOM's new Task Force Iron Gate is going after Iran's land borders and Caspian routes, on top of the naval blockade of Iranian ports in place since April 13. Iran's back door is already open: traders told RFE/RL that at least 6 million litres a day of Iranian fuel crosses into Pakistan in canisters on pickups and motorcycles, with boats running more to Gwadar. That's about 38,000 barrels a day, millions of barrels since the war began. Now 14+ tankers are massing at Kharg Island, Deputy FM Kazem Gharibabadi calls the blockade an "act of war," Iranian TV is floating putting journalists and civilians aboard, and an Iranian official warns that "if a single shot is fired," Gulf oil facilities become targets. Iran is betting the US won't sink a tanker full of civilians on live TV, and that the jerry cans keep flowing to world markets while Washington makes up its mind. I think it's right on both.

Great Timing: America's Cyber Chief Walks as the FBI and USDA Get Breached

Meanwhile, the man running DHS's terrorism-prevention office got the job at 22.

WASHINGTON — National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross is leaving the White House, confirmed on Nov. 19, and Trump has named Kirsten Davies to replace him. The same week, hackers got into the FBI's National Crime Information Center and a USDA system used for farmer payments. USDA says no data was taken, Trump confirmed the hack on Fox News, and nobody has been blamed. Over at DHS, the Center for Prevention Programs and Partnerships, its terrorism-prevention office, is run by Thomas Fugate, who ProPublica reported got the job at 22. Great fkn timing. The cyber chief walks mid-breach, and terrorism prevention is in the hands of a kid who just graduated.

Britain Says the Quiet Part Out Loud: We're Hacking Russia, China, Iran and North Korea

Asked if it's really happening, Britain's top cyber general said: "Absolutely."

LONDON — Gen. Sir Rob Magowan, head of the UK's Cyber and Specialist Operations Command, told Sky News on Nov. 20 that the National Cyber Force is running offensive operations against Russia, China, Iran and North Korea, "below the threshold of open conflict," in the grey zone. He added: "I wouldn't say we're at war." Under the UN Charter, which UN experts agreed in 2013, 2015 and 2021 applies to cyberspace, a cyber operation can be an illegal use of force, and an armed attack if its damage matches a conventional strike. Everyone knew Britain hacked back. Naming four countries on TV is a declaration. You don't announce attacks on Russia, China, Iran and North Korea and call it peace.

Ukraine Drops Putin's Bridge, Cuts Crimea's Lifeline

About 900 feet of road gone on the Kerch Bridge, the main link to Crimea.

KYIV — Over Nov. 16–19, Ukraine hit the Kerch Bridge, Russia's road and rail link to Crimea, with a Flamingo cruise missile and kamikaze drones. The bridge operator says about 900 feet of road surface was destroyed and an arch collapsed. Rail was suspended before service resumed, and the M-14 highway into Crimea was hit too. The Kremlin says it will rebuild. Putin opened this bridge by driving a truck across it himself. Now every shell for Crimea takes the long way round.

Russian Missiles Land in Poland. NATO Schedules a Meeting. Paper Tiger?

Warsaw calls it deliberate. Nobody has invoked Article 4, never mind Article 5.

WARSAW — On Nov. 20, two Kh-101 cruise missiles and a jet-powered drone came down in southeast Poland during a Russian mass strike on Ukraine. No one was hurt. PM Donald Tusk called it an "intentional provocation." NATO chief Mark Rutte called it a "grave violation" and promised to defend "every inch," and NATO held an urgent meeting on Nov. 23. Poland has not invoked Article 4, and analysts told Al Jazeera an Article 5 response is "probably not" coming. Russian missiles land in a NATO country and the answer is a meeting. If this triggers nothing, NATO may be a paper tiger, and Moscow just found out.

58 Reapers and Two Patriots, Says Iran. Silence, Says Washington.

The Pentagon denies one claim and says nothing about the rest.

MANAMA, BAHRAIN — On Nov. 21, the IRGC claimed it shot down its 58th US MQ-9 Reaper over the Strait of Hormuz and destroyed two US Patriot radars at bases in Bahrain. Bahrain says a civilian site was hit, and the Pentagon hasn't commented. Iran's army also says a missile exploded next to a US F-16 over the Gulf on Nov. 20, forcing an emergency landing; CENTCOM called that "FALSE." Back in March, an F-35 made an emergency landing after what sources told CNN was likely Iranian fire. A new Patriot battery costs about $1.1 billion, per the Congressional Research Service. I suspect Iran has locked onto an F-35 since, too. Washington denies the F-16 and goes quiet on the rest. When the Pentagon stops talking, I start counting.

The Oil War Goes Global: From the Baltic to Hormuz, One Fleet, Two Wars

Ukraine's drones reach Libya and the Baltic. Mines reach Hormuz. Same oil, same ships.

BENGHAZI, LIBYA — On Nov. 21–22, Ukraine hit the Russian corvette Merkury and the sanctioned cargo ship Angara off Benghazi, and the Defence Ministry-owned Sparta II in the Gulf of Finland. 301 ships sit on both the US Russia and Iran sanctions lists. The tanker Louise 1 shows how it works: it hauled Russian crude, took on 4.65 million barrels of Iranian oil in 2024, got hit by Ukrainian drones in the Black Sea in July, then by limpet mines near Hormuz on Oct. 29. Iran now says three more tankers hit mines, yet Brent sits near a three-month low around $89. Russia and Iran sell the same oil on the same ships. It's one war over one global commodity, fought on every ocean, and Ukraine has figured that out.

The Founders Sold at $83. You Bought at $97. It's $80 Today (Nov 24).

Four trading days from a record IPO to below the offer price.

NEW YORK — Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 on Nov. 18, raised a record $93.5 billion, and closed its first day on Nasdaq at $97.17. On Tuesday, Nov. 24, ANT traded around $80.46 at midday, down 8% on the day after touching $75.61, below the IPO price. The founders got $83. Retail paid $97. Your index fund buys whatever's left. The real selling hasn't even started.

Ebenezer Begins: Paramount Skydance Finds the Real Villains of Christmas, Charity and the Homeless

In Johnny Depp's new Scrooge, the men collecting for the poor are crooks and a beggar is a killer.

Written by: Camren

LOS ANGELES — Ebenezer, Paramount Skydance's new take on A Christmas Carol, directed by Ti West and starring Johnny Depp, opened No. 1 worldwide with $42 million after its Nov. 13 release. In this version, the two gentlemen collecting for the poor turn out to be embezzlers in a forged-will plot, a beggar in the prologue is a con artist, and Scrooge's mentor Fezziwig is stabbed to death by a street beggar: a Batman origin story, with the mentor in place of the parents. The LA Times called it "mean-spirited" and "very Ye Olde Fox News." The studio is run by David Ellison, son of one of the richest men on Earth. Dickens wrote the most famous story ever about a rich man learning to help the poor. A billionaire's studio rewrote it so the poor are the villains. Wink wink.
