8th Largest Economy in the World Sees Regime Change for the First Time in Decades

How Texas's changing guard will completely change its economy.

Democrat Gina Hinojosa unseated Greg Abbott, ending three decades of Republican governors, and James Talarico beat Ken Paxton with about 55% for the state's first Democratic Senate win since 1988. Texas goes from a billionaire technocratic candy land (Elon's factory town, beach buying) and oil barons to a state betting on renewables, and 40 years of educational failure finally get fixed by a teacher-turned-senator.

STFU Liberal… Wait, We Lost Texas, Kansas and South Carolina???

Democrats won the midterms. Now the real fight is inside the party.

Democrats flipped seven Senate seats (Kansas, Iowa, Texas, Ohio, Maine, North Carolina and South Carolina) for a 54–44 majority, and picked up 28 House seats. Mississippi is still too close to call, with Democrat Scott Colom ahead by just 43 votes. Now internal politics decide whether the same liberal machine keeps the wheel or a new crop of candidates takes over the leadership. Either way, Democrats control who gets confirmed to the federal bench for a whiiile.

ENSO ENSO ENSO: I Told You So

El Niño is already making our weather go haywire. Prepare for freezing rain.

Montreal hit 23°C on Nov 4, smashing the 1956 record of 18.3°C. The WMO says this El Niño could be one of the strongest since 1950 and will last into February 2027, and Intact Insurance warns of more freezing rain and ice this winter. Anyone who knows me knows I've been talking about this for over a year. The Ice Storm of '98 is gonna look like fkn peanuts. Prepare ;)

Your 401(k)s Are Citadel's Exit Liquidity

Who's left holding the bag before the bust? Check your retirement account.

SpaceX's $75B listing in June was the largest IPO ever, and Anthropic just filed for one that could raise up to $100B. 58% of US adults own stock, mostly through retirement accounts, and 97% of 401(k)s hold equities. Every mega-IPO gets funnelled straight into index funds and target-date plans at peak prices. When the bust comes, the billionaires are already out and your retirement is the bag.

Nuke the Mountain, Lose the War

Trump is eyeing Pickaxe Mountain. Not even a nuclear strike wins a war he's already losing.

Doha talks collapsed, and Trump says Pickaxe Mountain, Iran's deepest nuclear site, is on the table. On Oct 29, Sen. Chris Murphy, who had been briefed on the plan, went public: restarting the war after the midterms is "insane." Two days later, the 53-year-old had a stroke at his son's hockey game. Coincidence? I have my doubts. Either way, no bomb wins this. Winning means a ground war needing more boots than Iraq and Afghanistan combined (roughly 270,000 at their peaks), and that means a full national draft. Officially, 23 US troops have been killed. I suspect the real casualty count is closer to 200.

25 Countries and Counting: The Gen Z Revolt Goes Global

From Kathmandu to Cologne, the "sacrificed generation" is done waiting.

It started with French high schoolers blockading their schools in September. Since then, 550,000 marched in France and 120,000 in Germany, with Spain, Italy, Belgium, Portugal, Greece and Albania joining in, on top of Nepal, Madagascar, Morocco, Kenya, Peru and India. The grievances are local: rent, jobs, conscription. The anger is global, and governments that shrug it off won't last.

Analysis

Coup or Clean-Up? Poland's War Over Its Top Court

Police at the Tribunal, a president turning his plane around, and both sides calling the other criminals.

On Oct 30, Tusk's government suspended the leadership of the Constitutional Tribunal with police posted outside. President Nawrocki cut short a trip to Senegal and the right cried coup. Tusk says he's undoing PiS's court-packing. Either way, the EU's model democracy is now settling legal fights with police.

Opinion

Oui to the PQ, Non to the Referendum

Quebec voted for change, not a country, and St-Pierre Plamondon is about to learn the difference.

The PQ swept back to power on Oct 5 after 12 years out. PSPP's own estimate puts the referendum at $130 million, and only 20% of Quebecers want one. My call: he calls it anyway and probably hires an American firm to run it. Once Quebecers see the bill, and the loss it bought, it backfires and takes his mandate down with it.
